Tools · Color · Brush Size

Arc settings (start point → end point)

① Start point ② End point ③ Spacing point (dotted line)

Tap once to place the start point — you can change color and brush size first — then tap the end point: a solid line follows the start point’s color and size around the center; if both points are the same distance from the center you get a circular arc, if not, a spiral. For a dotted line, tap a third spacing point to set the gap; the dots blend from the start point’s look to the end point’s. Once the dashed preview is ready, the Draw It button under the disc works just like for a circle — no need to come back here. With the dashed preview up, tap Gear Teeth below to run progressive gear teeth along the arc (or a dashed circle): two gold spacing dots set the gap, and there is a small circle or line tooth at each end — the end tooth starts the same size as the start tooth, and each can be resized on its own. Before drawing, the start point, end point and the dashed line can all be dragged into place. Dragging the end point, the arc follows your finger’s direction: drag clockwise and it keeps going clockwise — a full turn or more; drag back the other way only if you want it to unwind.

Grid Ring settings

Tap the disc to set the grid ring’s size: the farther from the center you tap, the bigger the ring, and the cells spread along that big circle. After you let go, drag the two gold spacing dots to change the cell width, or change the spacing mode above. Tap Fill the Disc and the black-and-white cells repeat ring by ring, smaller each time, until the whole disc is filled. When it sits right, tap Draw It under the disc, then Spin — a grid disc gives the strongest illusion of all.

Text (the words stay still, the background spins)

110

Type your words, pick a color (use the palette above), then tap the red Add Text button to place the words right at the center of the disc (pressing Enter works too) — or tap the disc to place them where you tap. The words are a fixed layer on top of the disc: press Spin and the disc and artwork turn behind them while the words never move. Drag the words to move them; tap them to select and change the text, size or bold. The eraser cannot rub out words — use the delete button above. Saving an image includes the words. Clear wipes the words too, along with the artwork and the layers the words are on.

Layers · Strokes · Depth (a layer holds strokes; Finish Layer saves it)

14

Layers and strokes are separate: Draw It only adds one stroke to the current layer — freehand, circles, ellipses, rectangles, arcs and grid rings all work the same, and one layer can collect many strokes. Tap Finish Layer under the disc to save everything in the current layer as one layer. The list runs in stacking order, top layer first; under each layer its strokes are listed (that is the layer × stroke matrix). Tap a layer’s Edit and it becomes the current layer again: new strokes join it, and in Select mode you can tap any of its strokes on the disc to move or resize it on its own; each stroke can also be deleted from its list, or moved Forward / Back within the layer (which one covers which). The eye hides / shows, ↑ ↓ move the layer up or down the stack, and Delete removes the whole layer. Open Depth View and the layers on the disc are pulled apart upward, like a stack of plates separated, so you can see who sits on top of whom; the small picture below pulls them apart the same way.

No layers yet. Draw a dashed preview on the disc and tap Draw It — the stroke goes into the current layer (it does not become a layer by itself). Once the current layer holds enough strokes, tap Finish Layer to save it here as one layer. Each piece of text you place is still its own layer.

Demo Gallery · one tap draws it

Every demo looks striking once it spins. One tap draws it onto the disc — the disc stays at its current size and never grows off the screen (change the size yourself with the + / − above the disc) — and each demo is made of several layers, each holding several strokes: in the Layers panel above you can look layer by layer, with every stroke listed under its layer. Tap a layer’s Edit to select it and change any stroke inside it, or hide, reorder or delete a single layer; Undo takes the whole demo back off.

For the reversing illusion, tap Reversing Gear Ring or Spoke Wheel first: once it is drawn, press Spin, then drag the speed slider and try different speeds, slowly from slow to fast — at a certain speed the teeth and spokes seem to slow down, nearly stop, then turn backwards (the wagon-wheel effect); drag a little more and they turn forwards again. Rings with different tooth counts reverse at different speeds, so you can see the outer ring going backwards while the inner ring still goes forwards.

Checkerboard is rings of black-and-white checkerboard cells; spinning, the cells flow and flicker. Try the other demos at Fast 180, High 360 and Max 720 too: petals open and close, spirals seem to suck inward, wave rings rock in waves. The original Demo Pattern is still in the Canvas panel above, ready any time.

My Designs

Draw a picture on the disc, then tap Save Design in the Canvas panel above to keep it on this phone (up to 4 designs, still here after you close and reopen the page; this list always holds the 4 most recent, oldest first — saving a 5th drops the oldest and puts the new one last). Tap a card’s big picture or Load to put the design back on the disc; tap its name to rename it; tap Delete to remove it. Share Design copies a design code — send the code to a friend, and they paste it at the very bottom of the page to open your picture and spin it.
